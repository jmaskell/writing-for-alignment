# The appendix

Readers trust the narrative more when they can check it. The appendix lets them check without the main text slowing down to justify itself.

## What goes in it

Each part answers a question a sceptical reader would ask of a claim in the main text.

- **How the work was done:** sources, methods, sample sizes, counting rules and definitions. "A delivery counts as late when it arrives after the promised day; deliveries the customer rescheduled are left out."
- **The research behind the direction:** published studies and guidance, linked, with what each found.
- **The fuller reasoning** for each recommendation: the alternatives weighed and why they lost.

## What stays in the main text

The claim, the figure and the position. No method paragraphs, no provenance, no long justification. Where a reader may ask "how do you know?", the main text links to the part of the appendix that answers it: "Two in five orders ship late (method: Appendix A)."

## Where it goes

- **By default,** an appendix at the end of the same document, after the last main section, with one heading per part ("Appendix A: counting late orders").
- **A separate companion page** only when the appendix is long enough to slow the main page down, or when its readers are different, for example analysts checking the numbers.

## Not the evidence base

The evidence base is the author's working file and may carry IDs and file names. The appendix is written for readers, under the same writing rules as the main text: things named, never IDs; no file names or document versions.
