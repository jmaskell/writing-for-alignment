# Volunteer rota project: where we are and what we propose

## Why this matters

One in five volunteer shifts at Northside Food Bank ends in a no-show. Between June and August, 74 of 412 shifts (18%) were missed without notice. Every missed shift leaves a team short, and on Saturdays, when most of our families come, the rate is 31%. On weekdays it is between 12% and 15%.

The shortfall is felt most by the volunteers who do turn up. They cover the gaps, stay late and carry the extra work, and the coordinator spends much of each week finding replacements by message. Fixing no-shows is the quickest way to make the rota feel fair again.

## What we are building

The rota is run today from a shared spreadsheet and a WhatsApp group. Priya Shah, our volunteer coordinator, decided in August to replace both with one app. Volunteers will sign in with a link sent to their email, sign up for shifts, and be reminded before each one. The coordinator keeps the power to override any shift by hand.

## What we have learned

Three things shaped the plan.

First, the weekday and Saturday problems are different in size. Saturday is almost three times as bad as a typical weekday. A single fix for all days would leave Saturday behind.

Second, cost has narrowed the choice of reminder. We first planned text-message reminders. Priya decided on 2 September to use email only, because £40 a month is not in the budget. That decision stands.

Third, swapping is wanted but unfinished. Priya and I agree that volunteers should be able to swap shifts among themselves in the app. We have not settled who approves a swap or whether a swap needs a named replacement.

## What we propose

We propose two changes to how volunteers are reminded.

Email reminders go to every volunteer, 48 hours and 4 hours before each shift. They cost nothing and the app sends them automatically.

For Saturday shifts only, the shift lead also phones each volunteer the day before. We expect this to bring the Saturday rate down to the weekday level. The calls are the part we most want your view on. They take about two hours of the lead's time a week.

We propose these as the start, not as a final answer. The November pilot with the Saturday and Tuesday teams will show whether they work.

## What this does not solve

The proposal does not settle how swaps are approved. It does not tell us why Saturday is worse, only that it is. And it does nothing for the number of people who volunteer in the first place.

## What we are less sure of

We have no evidence yet that a phone call works better than an email. The call helps because it is personal, and our shift leads know most of the regulars by name. Email is cheaper but easier to ignore.

## What we need to agree

We would like your view on three themes:

- Are email for everyone and a call for Saturdays the right place to start?
- Is the November pilot the right way to find out?
- How much of the shift lead's time are we willing to commit to the calls?

## Next steps

Build the email reminders and the sign-up flow in October. Run the pilot in November. Report back to the trustees in December with the no-show rate for the pilot teams against the same weeks last year.

## Appendix: how we counted

A shift counts as a no-show when the volunteer neither attends nor swaps 24 hours ahead. Cancellations made more than 24 hours ahead are not counted. The figures come from the coordinator's spreadsheet for June to August 2026.
