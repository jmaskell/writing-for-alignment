# Writing briefings

Load the `writing-for-alignment` skill first with the Skill tool, unless it is already loaded, and follow its process: its gates, playback, evidence base, writing rules, check and rehearsal all apply. This skill adds the briefing's outline and its failure checks. At step 4, use `templates/briefing.md` (at the plugin root, two folders up) as the outline.

## When it fits

A piece of work is under way or has reached a milestone, and readers who weren't in it need to know where it stands and what is proposed next. If the readers must make one decision, a decision memo fits better; if the work is research to report, a research synthesis does. Say so at the playback.

## The readers' questions, in order

These become the sections and the step 4 plan. Name each section for what it says.

1. **Why does this matter?** The problem in concrete, measured terms. It comes straight after the title: no summary, scope or background above it.
2. **What is it?** The work, in a few lines.
3. **What did we learn?** Organised by what each stage taught us, never by date.
4. **What do we propose?** Open by saying it is proposed and not agreed. State the aims every change must serve. Tie each change to a finding from section 3. Where there were options, name the one you recommend and the reason it beats the others. Say what the proposal does not solve.
5. **What are we less sure of?** Open points, each with what would settle it.
6. **What we need to agree, and what happens next.**

## Where it ends

The last section before the appendix asks readers what they agree and disagree with: alignment, not approval. It holds a few broad themes, not a list of low-level questions for named people. The next steps go at the end of that same section, so the briefing never ends on a summary, a list of open questions or a plan alone.

## Briefing failure checks

Add these to the independent check (step 6), and fix each one found:

- Chronology instead of lessons: sections that tell what happened when, not what it taught.
- A proposal not tied to an earlier finding.
- An option recommended with no reason, or options listed with no recommendation.
- No "what it does not solve".
- The last main section is next steps, open questions or a summary, with no ask for agreement.
