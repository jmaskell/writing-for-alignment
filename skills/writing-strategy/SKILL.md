# Writing strategy

Load the `writing-for-alignment` skill first with the Skill tool, unless it is already loaded, and follow its process: its gates, playback, evidence base, writing rules, check and rehearsal all apply. This skill adds the strategy kernel and its failure checks. At step 4, use `templates/strategy.md` (at the plugin root, two folders up) as the outline.

The kernel is from Richard Rumelt, [*Good Strategy Bad Strategy*](https://profilebooks.com/work/good-strategy-bad-strategy/) (Profile Books, 2011).

## When it fits

Readers must agree how to meet a challenge over the coming period. If they must make one decision, a decision memo fits better; if they need to know where work stands, a briefing does. Say so at the playback.

## No challenge, no strategy

The diagnosis comes from the author or the sources, never from you. When the material holds goals, targets or plans but never says what challenge they answer, say so at the playback and ask the author what the challenge is. Never infer one from the goals or write the diagnosis yourself; until the author names it, the diagnosis is open and drafting waits.

## The kernel, in order

These become the sections and the step 4 plan. Name each section for what it says.

1. **What is really going on? (diagnosis)** A good one names the one or two critical obstacles, with their evidence, and makes a messy situation simple enough to act on.
2. **What is our approach? (guiding policy)** Its own section, between the diagnosis and the actions. A good one is a few sentences on how we will meet the challenge: what we lean on, and what we will stop or not do. It is not a goal, a target or a list of actions; each action below should follow from it.
3. **What will we do, and how do the actions fit together? (coherent actions)** Each action says which part of the policy it carries out. Say how the actions reinforce each other. Drop any that follow from nothing.
4. **First moves.** The few actions that start now, and who leads each. Targets, if readers expect them, go here as measures of progress, never as the strategy.

## Where it ends

On the coherent actions and the first moves, before the appendix. No closing summary.

## Strategy failure checks

Rumelt's signs of bad strategy, plus two kernel checks. Add them to the independent check (step 6), and fix each one found:

- Fluff: grand words that sound like strategy and say nothing ("world-class", "best-in-class").
- Goals presented as strategy: a target or an ambition where the approach should be.
- A diagnosis that names no real challenge, or one the author never gave.
- No distinct guiding policy: the diagnosis runs straight into a list of actions.
- Actions that don't follow from the policy, or don't reinforce each other.
